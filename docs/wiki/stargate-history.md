# The Stargate franchise

Stargate began as a 1994 film about a ring of alien metal that opens a wormhole to another world. It grew into three live-action series, an animated series, two films on DVD, a web series and a long line of games and books, and it is the world SGCraft brings into Minecraft.

## The film

*Stargate* opened in the United States on October 28, 1994. Roland Emmerich directed it, and he wrote it with Dean Devlin. Kurt Russell plays Colonel Jack O'Neil and James Spader plays Daniel Jackson, an Egyptologist who works out how to use a ring found at Giza in 1928. The two lead a team through the gate to Abydos, a desert world where Ra, an alien who poses as a god, keeps humans as slaves to mine the mineral his technology runs on.

The film was made by Carolco Pictures, Le Studio Canal+ and Centropolis Film Productions, and MGM released it in the United States. It took about $196 million worldwide on a budget of about $55 million. Devlin meant it as the first of a trilogy, and he talked about sequels for years afterward, but none was made.

## Stargate SG-1

*Stargate SG-1* picked up the story a year after the film. Brad Wright and Jonathan Glassner created it, and it premiered on Showtime on July 27, 1997. Richard Dean Anderson took over the role of Jack O'Neill (now with two Ls), with Michael Shanks as Daniel Jackson, Amanda Tapping as Samantha Carter and Christopher Judge as Teal'c. Don S. Davis played General Hammond, and later seasons added Corin Nemec, Ben Browder, Beau Bridges and Claudia Black.

The show is set at Stargate Command, a secret Air Force base under Cheyenne Mountain in Colorado. Teams travel through the gate to other worlds, meet allies such as the Tok'ra, the Asgard and the Tollan, and fight the Goa'uld, parasites who rule the galaxy as false gods. The gate network turns out to be the work of the Ancients, a people who left the galaxy long ago. It was filmed in and around Vancouver, British Columbia.

Showtime aired the first five seasons. The Sci Fi Channel picked the show up from season six, which began on June 7, 2002. The producers often expected each season to be the last and wrote finales to match, but the show kept being renewed. It ran ten seasons and 214 episodes. The final episode, "Unending", first aired on Sky1 in the United Kingdom on March 13, 2007, and on the Sci Fi Channel on June 22, 2007.

## Stargate Infinity

*Stargate Infinity* is an animated series about a veteran officer, Gus Bonner, and a group of young recruits who flee through the gate after he is framed for an ambush, and who cannot go home until they clear their names. Eric Lewald and Michael Maliani created it, and DIC Entertainment made it with Les Studios Tex and MGM Television. It ran for one season of 26 episodes on Fox's FoxBox block, starting on September 14, 2002, and ending in 2003. Its writers had no part in the live-action shows, and Brad Wright has said it should not be counted as canon.

## Stargate Atlantis

*Stargate Atlantis* was the first live-action spin-off. Brad Wright and Robert C. Cooper created it, and it aired on the Sci Fi Channel from July 16, 2004, to January 9, 2009: five seasons of 20 episodes, 100 in all.

An expedition from Earth dials an eight-symbol address to the Pegasus galaxy and finds Atlantis, a city the Ancients left sunk in the ocean of the planet Lantea about 10,000 years before, when they fled the Wraith. The team, led by John Sheppard, makes the city its base and fights the Wraith, who feed on humans. Pegasus has its own design of gate, with lit symbols in place of a spinning ring.

## The films on DVD

When SG-1 ended, its story finished in two films made for DVD.

- *Stargate: The Ark of Truth* came out on DVD in North America on March 11, 2008. Robert C. Cooper wrote and directed it. It ends the story of the Ori, the enemy of SG-1's last two seasons.
- *Stargate: Continuum* came out on DVD and Blu-ray on July 29, 2008. Brad Wright wrote it and Martin Wood directed it. It is a time travel story: history is changed so that the Stargate program never existed, and the team has to set it right.

A third SG-1 film, centered on Jack O'Neill, was put on hold. An Atlantis film, *Stargate: Extinction*, was written but shelved at the end of 2010. Neither was made.

## Stargate Universe

*Stargate Universe* took the franchise in a darker direction. Brad Wright and Robert C. Cooper created it, and it aired on Syfy from October 2, 2009, to May 9, 2011, for two seasons of 20 episodes each, plus 34 short webisodes.

A group of soldiers, scientists and civilians escapes an attack by dialing a nine-symbol address and finds itself aboard *Destiny*, a ship the Ancients launched long ago, billions of light-years from the Milky Way. They cannot steer it or go home, and they have to keep it running as it follows its old course. *Destiny*'s gates have a ring that turns as a whole. Syfy announced on December 16, 2010, that there would be no third season, so the show ended on an open note.

## Stargate Origins

*Stargate Origins* is a prequel web series about Catherine Langford, whose father found the gate at Giza in 1928. Ellie Gall plays her. Mercedes Bryce Morgan directed all ten episodes, each about 10 minutes long. They came out on Stargate Command between February 15 and March 8, 2018. The episodes were cut together into a 104-minute film, *Stargate Origins: Catherine*, released on June 19, 2018.

## Stargate Command

MGM announced Stargate Command at San Diego Comic-Con on July 20, 2017, together with *Origins*, and opened it that fall. It was a streaming service for Stargate alone: it carried the film, the three live-action series and the DVD films, along with interviews and behind-the-scenes shows for subscribers.

MGM closed the service on December 31, 2019. The interview series and other original videos moved to the free [Stargate Command YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@StargateCommand), which MGM still runs. The series and films did not move there.

## Games and books

Stargate has been adapted for games several times.

- *Stargate Worlds*, an online role-playing game, was canceled during development.
- *Stargate: Resistance*, an online third-person shooter, came out on February 10, 2010, and was later discontinued.
- *Stargate SG-1: Unleashed*, an episodic adventure game made by Arkalis Interactive with MGM, came out on iOS in 2013, with a second episode later that year.
- *Stargate: Timekeepers*, a real-time tactics game, came out on PC on January 23, 2024. CreativeForge Games began it, and Slitherine published it.

The books started with Bill McCay's five novels from Signet, which went on from the film. Ashley McConnell wrote four SG-1 novels for Roc from 1998 to 2001. From the middle of the 2000s the British publisher Fandemonium released licensed SG-1, Atlantis and Universe novels and short story collections, written to fit the shows' continuity and approved by MGM. There have also been several runs of comics.

## The franchise today

Amazon bought MGM in March 2022, so Stargate now belongs to Amazon MGM Studios.

On November 19, 2025, Amazon MGM Studios announced a new live-action series for Prime Video. Martin Gero, who had written for and produced all three live-action shows, created it and was to run it. Brad Wright and Joseph Mallozzi joined as producers, and Roland Emmerich and Dean Devlin returned as executive producers, along with Joby Harold and Tory Tunnell of Safehouse Pictures. Gero said the show would carry on the same story. "It is not a reboot," he said, as [GateWorld](https://www.gateworld.net/news/2025/11/amazon-greenlights-new-stargate-series/) reported. The writers' room met in early 2026, and the show had begun casting and design work for filming that fall.

On June 2, 2026, *Variety* reported that Amazon MGM Studios had dropped the series. According to the report, executives worried that it would not reach far enough past the existing fans. Fans answered with the Save Stargate campaign (see [The community](community.md#save-stargate)).

Amazon did not drop Stargate itself. In September 2026, *Deadline* reported that the studio was looking for a new writer. On October 9, 2026, *Variety* reported that Jac Schaeffer, who created *WandaVision* and *Agatha All Along*, is developing a new Stargate series for Amazon MGM Studios, with Joby Harold and Tory Tunnell as executive producers. At the time of writing, no title, story or cast has been announced, and the project has not been given a green light. [GateWorld](https://www.gateworld.net/) follows the news as it comes.

You can still watch the older shows. In February 2026 all ten seasons of SG-1 came back to Netflix in the United States and other countries, and in the United States all three live-action series stream on Prime Video.

## From the show to the mod

SGCraft takes its pieces from the shows. Each one below links to the page that tells you how it works in the game.

- **The gate.** A ring of symbols with chevrons around it, made by the Ancients and found on world after world. In SGCraft you build the ring from blocks and form it with a base. See [Building gates](building-gates.md).
- **Chevrons.** In the shows, seven symbols reach a gate in the same galaxy, an eighth reaches another galaxy, and nine reach *Destiny*. SGCraft follows the same idea: a gate starts with 7 chevrons for its own world, and upgrades add an 8th and a 9th to reach further. See [Building gates](building-gates.md#chevrons-and-upgrades) and [Addresses](addresses.md).
- **The point of origin.** Every address ends with the symbol of the gate you dial from. SGCraft's point of origin is `@`, Giza, the place where the first film's gate was found. See [Addresses](addresses.md#point-of-origin).
- **The DHD.** The dial-home device is the pedestal of symbols beside a gate. Stargate Command had no DHD and dialed with a computer; SGCraft has DHDs as well as handheld dialers and a command. See [The DHD](dhd.md) and [Dialing](dialing.md).
- **The kawoosh.** The burst of energy that shoots out of a gate as the wormhole forms, and destroys what stands in its way. SGCraft's kawoosh harms anything in front of the gate and clears blocks. See [Dialing](dialing.md#the-kawoosh).
- **The iris.** Stargate Command fitted a metal shield across its gate, so travelers who have not sent the right code cannot come through. Teams send that code with a GDO. SGCraft has both. See [The iris](iris.md).
- **The MALP.** The Mobile Analytic Laboratory Probe is the wheeled robot the teams send through first, to look at a world before anyone steps through. In SGCraft you drive it from a console and watch its camera. See [The MALP](malp.md).
- **Naquadah.** The mineral the gates are made of, and the source of their power in many stories. In SGCraft naquadah ore generates underground and fuels the DHD. See [World generation](worldgen.md#naquadah-ore).
- **The Tok'ra.** A group of symbiotes who fight the Goa'uld from inside and became Earth's allies. In SGCraft they are villagers who watch over the gates the world builds and trade crystals. See [World generation](worldgen.md#tokra-villagers).
- **Pegasus and Universe gates.** Atlantis's gates light their symbols in turn, and *Destiny*'s gates turn their whole ring. SGCraft has both types beside the Milky Way gate, each with its own glyphs. See [Building gates](building-gates.md#gate-types).
- **Supergates.** SG-1's later seasons showed a gate big enough for starships. SGCraft's supergates can be built up to 61 blocks across. See [Building gates](building-gates.md#supergates).
